Method, sources and limits — worth reading

How the scores are produced. Every question carries a difficulty level 1–5. A 3PL-style item response model is fitted: P(correct) = c + (1−c)·logit⁻¹(θ − b), where b is the item difficulty (level 1–5 mapped to −1.8 … +1.8 logits), c is the guessing floor (1 ÷ number of options, so 0.25 for a four-option item and 0.20 for the five-option OC maths items), and θ is ability, estimated by EAP over a normal reference prior.

What the percentile means. Ability in the reference group is taken as N(0, σ), so the percentile is Φ(θ/σ). For the age comparison the expected θ is shifted by (age − reference age) × growth per year: the same performance therefore scores higher for a younger child.

The important caveat. The reference distribution is a model built on this app's own difficulty ladder — it is not measured NAPLAN norm data. The numbers are meaningful for tracking direction and spread over time. They are not a substitute for an official scaled score. Calibrate the parameters opposite once you have a real result.

EAP shrinkage. Estimates are pulled toward the mean: in simulation, a child whose true θ is +2 is recovered at about +1.72 over 60 items. At the extremes his real ability is therefore a little higher than shown. The ±1 standard error band is drawn on the ability chart, and it narrows as he answers more.

Minimum data. No percentile is shown for a topic with fewer than 8 answers.

Curriculum and test sources, checked September 2026.

  • Australian Curriculum v9 Mathematics, Year 2 and Year 3 content descriptions — the level ladder is mapped to these codes, visible in the topic table. Year 2 covers numbers to 1000, halves/quarters/eighths by repeated halving, ×2 facts with doubling and halving, informal units, calendars and clock to the quarter-hour. Thirds and fifths, formal units, chance and the 3–9 times tables are Year 3 or later, and are marked as such.
  • NAPLAN (nap.edu.au): Year 3 sits Reading 45 min, Conventions of Language 45 min (spelling, then grammar and punctuation) and Numeracy 40 min. The online test is tailored: everyone starts on testlet A and moves to an easier or harder testlet based on it. This app's NAPLAN mode reproduces that branching.
  • NSW Opportunity Class placement test (education.nsw.gov.au): Reading 40 min; Thinking Skills 30 questions in 30 min with four options; Mathematical Reasoning 35 questions in 40 min with five options. Content comes from the NSW curriculum up to Year 4, and the paper is a fixed form — so OC mode here does not adapt, and its maths questions offer five options.
